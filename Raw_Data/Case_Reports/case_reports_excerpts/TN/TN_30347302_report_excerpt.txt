A 12-year-old girl had presented with several episodes of severe facial pain that had occurred over the course of 3 weeks. The patient’s pain was described as intermittent, explosive attacks of lancinating pain. The affected areas included the jaw, cheek, ear, temporal, and preauricular regions. The trigger mechanism for these episodes included chewing and a light touch from a slight breeze or raindrops. She also experienced pain with bumps or vibration when riding in a car.
She had initially been treated with a course of prednisone but experienced only minimal relief. She had also received several combinations of therapies, including indomethacin prochlorperazine, and chlorpromazine, all of which were ineffective in reducing the frequency and intensity of the pain episodes. The use of carbamazepine, naproxen, gabapentin, and valacyclovir also did not yield any benefit. These episodes were severely limiting, preventing her from attending school or participating in cheerleading. Her medical history was unremarkable. The physical examination did not reveal any significant neurological findings, other than notable allodynia around the left temporal region and extending into the mandibular and maxillary regions.
