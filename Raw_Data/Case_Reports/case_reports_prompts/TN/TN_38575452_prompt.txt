A 58-year-old male presented for an examination in the Orofacial Pain/Temporomandibular Disorders/Sleep-Disordered Breathing (OFP/TMD/SDB) clinic in the Oral Medicine department. The chief complaint was pain in the right supraorbital region. It was described as a continuous shooting pain with paroxysmal, stabbing flare-ups. Any movement, particularly vibration, aggravated this pain. Shaving, covering the affected area with any clothing, talking loudly, or smiling triggered brief and severe pain. The patient did not report or exhibit any muscle weakness or other neurological defects. Clinical history revealed that the patient had been experiencing these symptoms for the last 25 years, and they were recurring cyclically with periods of quiescence for a few weeks between pain episodes. However, the pain has been consistent for the past ten months when he reported to us. The patient had undergone neurologic consultation 5 years prior, where all other causes of ophthalmic pain were ruled out through clinical exam, imaging studies (brain magnetic resonance image, computed tomography, and dental radiographs), and serological assessment. Imaging did not show any vascular compression of the trigeminal nerve.
