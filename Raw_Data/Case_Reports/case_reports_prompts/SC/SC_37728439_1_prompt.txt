A patient in her late 60s presented with a BRAF V600E mutated melanoma with lymph node and brain metastases. Her disease had previously progressed under ICIs and BRAF/MEK inhibitors (BRAFi/MEKi). In March 2021, we started triplet therapy (triplet) with atezolizumab, vemurafenib, and cobimetinib.
In March 2022, after 12 months on triplet with 24 atezolizumab infusions, she presented with fever, fatigue, nausea, and right upper abdominal pain. Laboratory tests revealed elevated C-reactive protein and interleukin-6 and a grade 1 (G1) cholestatic liver injury according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events v5.0. Her liver-related history was unremarkable. Computed tomography (CT) detected newly accentuated intrahepatic bile ducts without obstruction. Given inflammation without evidence of infection, a cytokine release syndrome was suspected. BRAFi/MEKi were withheld, and high-dose CS was initiated (Fig. 1A). Biliary enzymes persisted at G1 ALP and G3 GGT elevation; however, upon resolution of symptoms and inflammation within a week, she was dismissed. As CS was tapered to prednisone (PDN) 20 mg/d, she received the 25th atezolizumab infusion. Symptoms reappeared and laboratory tests revealed G3 cholestatic liver injury. Magnetic resonance imaging and cholangiopancreatography (MRI/MRCP) showed patchy enhancement of the liver parenchyma and intrahepatic bile duct dilatation.
