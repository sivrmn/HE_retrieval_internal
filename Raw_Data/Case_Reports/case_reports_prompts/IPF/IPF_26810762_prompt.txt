A 55-year-old male, non-smoker without any occupational exposure was referred to pulmonology department. after being diagnosed with autoimmune negative anterior uveitis. He complained of progressive dyspnoea (grade I–II of Medical Research Council (MRC)) and non-productive cough. He had a history of bronchiectasis secondary to pulmonary tuberculosis 30 years ago, as well as oesophageal spasm as his only other relevant history. His physical examination revealed bilateral basal crackles but otherwise unremarkable. 
Lung function test in his first consultation were as follows: forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1), 60% predicted; forced vital capacity (FVC), 58% predicted; FEV1/FVC, 84%; diffusing capacity of the lungs for carbon monoxide (DLco), 62% predicted; DLco/alveolar volume (VA), 110% predicted; total lung capacity (TLC), 74% predicted; and residual volume, 120% predicted. Blood gas parameters were within the reference range values: pH 7.41; partial pressure of arterial oxygen, 85 mmHg; partial pressure of arterial carbon dioxide, 40 mmHg; oxygen saturation, 98%. Chest radiography and chest CT showed bilateral apical pleural thickening associated with bronchiectasis, parenchymal bands, distortion and shrinkage in both upper lobes. The left lower lobe was also affected in a similar way. Given the non-diagnostic radiological pattern and progressive decline in his respiratory function tests, it was decided to perform a video-assisted thoracoscopic surgical lung biopsy of the right upper lobe. 
Severe subpleural fibrosis associated with septal elastosis was observed histopathologically. Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection or fungus were ruled out with microbiological study.
