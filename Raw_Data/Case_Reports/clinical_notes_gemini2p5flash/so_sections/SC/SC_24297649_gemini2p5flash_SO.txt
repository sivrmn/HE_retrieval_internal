Johnathan Doe, a 16-year-old male, was referred for acute abdominal pain, elevated pancreatic enzymes, and abnormal liver function tests (LFTs).

S: Subjective
The patient, Johnathan Doe, is a 16-year-old male presenting with a history of recurrent, severe, and unremitting abdominal pain. The pain has progressed over the last 2.5 years since his initial diagnosis. He reports associated vomiting, dehydration, and significant weight loss, which has led to a severe deterioration of his nutritional status despite receiving parenteral nutrition. His pain is poorly controlled, with only temporary relief from a celiac nerve block and two previous pancreatic stent placements.

O: Objective
Labs: Elevated pancreatic enzymes and abnormal LFTs. Elevated serum IgG, with normal IgG4 subclass. Genetic testing for mutations in CFTR, PRSS1, and SPINK1 was negative.

Imaging:

MRCP: Initial MRCP revealed multifocal stenosis with post-stenotic dilatation of the intra- and extrahepatic biliary ducts. A hypointense signal in the body of the pancreas was suspicious for chronic pancreatitis.

Follow-up MRCP (with secretin injection): No improvement in pancreatic duct narrowing.

Procedures:

Endoscopy: Diagnosis of ulcerative colitis.

ERCP: Demonstrated mild changes of chronic pancreatitis and an irregular main pancreatic duct with slightly dilated side branches. A pancreatic sphincterotomy was performed.

Celiac Nerve Block: Provided temporary relief.

Pancreatic Stent: Provided temporary relief on two occasions.

Medications:

Prednisone (40 mg daily for 6 weeks, then tapered).

Mesalamine.

Ursodiol.

Pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy.

Proton pump inhibitor.
