Subjective
The patient is a new 18-year-old female presenting with a history of bilateral hip pain and swelling that began at menarche and has progressively worsened. She describes the pain as acute and inflammatory, occurring primarily during the premenstrual period. She notes a progressive increase in the volume and rigidity of both hips. There is no history of trauma to the area. She reports a history of oligophrenia, alopecia, and bilateral hearing impairment. She denies any recent weight loss, fever, or other systemic signs of illness.

Objective
The patient has a noticeable waddling gait. She has limited and painful mobility of both hips, with a range of motion of 0-0-75°. On physical exam, there are hard, prominent bilateral masses in the hip area. Plain pelvic radiography and a CT scan of the pelvis and thighs were reviewed. The images show extensive soft tissue calcifications enveloping both hips and thighs. There is no involvement of the surrounding bone structures, and no other anomalous tissue was noted at the site of the calcifications.
