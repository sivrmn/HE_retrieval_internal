Patient: Aphneus Piriton

Subjective
Chief Complaint: Multiple bony swellings on the scalp, neck, and thorax.

History of Present Illness (HPI):

Aphneus is a 7-year-old boy who presents to the pediatric surgery clinic with a 3-year history of gradually worsening hard, bony swellings. The swellings initially appeared on his scalp after a fall at age 4. Over the past couple of years, he developed nodules on the right side of his neck, which were initially soft but gradually hardened, restricting his neck mobility on the right.  One year ago, after a fall from his bicycle, he developed palpable hard nodules on his right lateral thoracic cage. As these hardened, they began to restrict his chest expansion, more so on the right side.

The parents report no other systemic complaints. They have sought multiple consultations and received varied advice.

Past Medical/Surgical History:

Birth History: First-born, full-term baby boy.

Developmental Milestones: Normal.

Congenital Anomalies: Bilateral hallux valgus deformity and aplasia of the distal phalanges of both thumbs.

Trauma History: Multiple falls, triggering the development of swellings.

Family History:

Parents are in a consanguineous marriage.

No family history of skeletal or connective tissue disease.
